\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced Regret, an open CFR$^+$ solver with a reproducible empirical
analysis framework. Our main empirical findings, measured on the
$K\!=\!50$, $\{0.25,0.50,0.75\}$-pot, $100$\,bb HUNL flop subgame:
CFR$^+$ exhibits the monotone $O(1/T)$-style decay characteristic of
regret-matching$^+$ with linear iterate weighting, while vanilla CFR's
unweighted average is best-response noisy and plateaus in an
oscillation band; the asymptotic separation is real but materializes
only beyond the typical $T\!=\!1{,}000$ budget on a game of this size.
Card-abstraction error against a $K\!=\!200$ proxy is largely monotone
above $K\!=\!10$ on dry and semi-wet textures and effectively flat by
$K\!=\!50$ on the semi-wet flop; the $K\!=\!10$ regime is too coarse for
the metric to be meaningful. Action-abstraction error decreases
monotonically in the bet-size set, with the largest single drop coming
from adding a pot-sized bet ($\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$ to
$\{0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}$ cuts $\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}$ from $0.91$ to
$0.42$\,bb). On the theoretical side we sketch a framing of CFR$^+$ as
Optimistic FTRL in self-play that situates the regret-flooring step
within the broader online-learning literature.

\section*{Reproducibility statement}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

All empirical numbers, tables, and figures in this paper are regenerable
from a single artifact, \texttt{paper/experiments/results.json}, which
contains the raw exploitability trajectories for all four experiments
and the $T\!=\!3000$ extension used in Figure~\ref{fig:cfr_vs_cfr+}. The
end-to-end pipeline is:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{cargo build --release --bin run\_experiments}
  \item \texttt{./target/release/run\_experiments --out
        paper/experiments/expN.json --experiments N} for
        $N\!\in\!\{1,2,3,4\}$ (parallelizable across cores; total
        wall-clock $\sim\!4$\,h on an 8-core M-series MacBook).
  \item \texttt{bash paper/experiments/finalize.sh} --- merges the
        per-experiment JSONs into \texttt{results.json}, re-renders
        Figures~\ref{fig:cfr_vs_cfr+}--\ref{fig:action-abs} at
        $300$\,DPI, and rewrites the tables and prose paragraphs in
        Sections~\ref{sec:convergence}--\ref{sec:abstraction-error} from
        the JSON.
\end{enumerate}

Source for the solver, the experiment driver, the plotting / prose
scripts, and the \LaTeX{} sources for this paper is at
\url{https://github.com/arpjw/regret}. Card-abstraction Monte Carlo
rollouts use seed \texttt{0xC0FFEE} throughout the experiments; CFR$^+$
updates are deterministic given the seed, so re-running the pipeline
reproduces the JSON byte-for-byte.
