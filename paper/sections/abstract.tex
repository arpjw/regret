We present Regret, an open-source CFR$^+$ solver for Heads-Up No-Limit Hold'em
(HUNL) paired with an interactive web interface for visualizing strategy
convergence and hand-range analysis. Beyond the implementation, we make three
research contributions: (1) an empirical characterization of CFR$^+$
convergence rates as a function of game abstraction granularity, extending
known theoretical bounds to practical parameter regimes; (2) a systematic
measurement of abstraction error---the gap between the abstracted-game Nash
equilibrium and the full-game Nash equilibrium---as a function of card-bucket
count and bet-size discretization; and (3) a formal connection between
CFR$^+$ and Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL), clarifying the role of
regret flooring in accelerating convergence. All results are reproducible
via the Regret web interface.
