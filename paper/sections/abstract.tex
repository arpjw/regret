We present Regret, an open-source CFR$^+$ solver for Heads-Up No-Limit
Hold'em (HUNL) paired with a web interface for visualizing strategy
convergence and hand-range analysis. We use the framework to characterize
two empirical phenomena that the theoretical CFR$^+$ literature does not
fully cover at practical iteration budgets: (1) on a $50$-bucket,
$3$-bet-size HUNL flop subgame at $100$\,bb stacks, the linear iterate
weighting that powers CFR$^+$'s asymptotic $O(1/T)$ rate concentrates
its average on still-converging recent strategies, so vanilla CFR's
unweighted average is BR-noisy but transiently lower-exploitability
($0.150$\,bb at $T\!=\!1{,}000$) than CFR$^+$ ($1.28$\,bb), with the
crossover lying several thousand iterations beyond the typical budget
(CFR$^+$ reaches $0.86$\,bb by $T\!=\!3000$ while vanilla CFR continues
to oscillate in $[0.08, 0.41]$\,bb); and (2) measured against a
$K\!=\!200$ proxy, card-abstraction error decreases roughly monotonically
in $K$ above $K\!=\!10$ on the dry flop ($2.45 \to 1.66 \to 1.10$\,bb at
$K\!\in\!\{25, 50, 100\}$), while action-abstraction error is cleanly
monotone in the bet-size set size --- $\{0.5\}$ alone is exploitable for
$2.16$\,bb, the $\{0.25,0.50,0.75\}$ default for $0.91$\,bb, and adding
a pot-sized bet halves that to $0.42$\,bb. Beyond these measurements we
sketch a formal connection between CFR$^+$ and FTRL that situates the
regret-flooring step within the broader online-learning literature.
All numbers in the paper are reproducible from a single results file
via \texttt{paper/experiments/finalize.sh}.
