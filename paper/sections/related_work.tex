\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Superhuman poker agents.} Libratus \citep{brown2017libratus} and
DeepStack \citep{moravcik2017deepstack} are the canonical superhuman HUNL
agents. Both rely on a blueprint strategy plus real-time subgame solving.
Neither system's abstraction details are public.

\paragraph{Open-source CFR.} Slumbot \citep{jackson2013} is the strongest
openly available bot, with a single-threaded CFR implementation and limited
abstraction analysis.

\paragraph{Theoretical foundations.} CFR$^+$ \citep{tammelin2014} introduces
regret flooring with theoretical analysis but limited empirical
characterization. \citet{waugh2009pathologies} document that coarser
abstractions can produce \emph{more} exploitable strategies---``abstraction
pathologies''---motivating systematic measurement.

\paragraph{Online learning.} The connection between CFR-style regret
minimization and Follow-the-Regularized-Leader is implicit in earlier work;
\citet{hazan2022oco} provides the textbook framework we use here.
\citet{rakhlin2013optimistic} introduce Optimistic FTRL, whose update we
relate directly to CFR$^+$'s regret-flooring step in Section~\ref{sec:ol}.

\paragraph{Gap.} No existing work provides an open, reproducible framework
for empirically measuring the relationship between abstraction granularity
and solution quality across a range of HUNL configurations. Regret fills
this gap.
