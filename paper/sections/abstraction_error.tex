\section{Abstraction error analysis}
\label{sec:abstraction-error}

\subsection{Definition}

Abstraction error is the difference between the exploitability of the
abstracted-game Nash strategy evaluated in the full game vs.\ the
abstracted game. Formally,
\[
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}(K)
    \;=\; \mathrm{expl}_{\mathrm{full}}(\bar{\sigma}^\star_K)
       - \mathrm{expl}_{\mathrm{abs}}(\bar{\sigma}^\star_K),
\]
where $\bar{\sigma}^\star_K$ is the Nash strategy of the $K$-bucket
abstracted game.

\subsection{Measurement approach}

We cannot directly compute $\mathrm{expl}_{\mathrm{full}}$ for the complete
HUNL game. Instead, we use a finer abstraction ($200$ buckets) as a proxy
for the ``true'' game and measure how strategies from coarser abstractions
perform when evaluated within it.

\subsection{Card-abstraction error}

We solve to convergence at each bucket count and evaluate the resulting
strategy in the $200$-bucket proxy game.

\textbf{Expected results.} Abstraction error decreases monotonically with
bucket count but exhibits diminishing returns above $50$--$100$ buckets.
Error is more pronounced on wet boards (coordinated texture) where hand
equity distributions are more sensitive to bucketing resolution.

\textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:card-abs} (placeholder).} Abstraction error vs.\
bucket count for dry, semi-wet, and wet board textures.

\subsection{Action-abstraction error}

We fix card abstraction at $50$ buckets and vary the bet-size set,
evaluating exploitability in a richer-action evaluation game.

\textbf{Expected results.} Missing pot-sized bets introduces more error
than missing small bets, particularly on the flop where pot-size bets are
part of the polarization structure of GTO play.
