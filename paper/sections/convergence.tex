\section{Convergence analysis}
\label{sec:convergence}

\subsection{CFR vs CFR$^+$}

We compare vanilla CFR and CFR$^+$ across $1{,}000$ iterations on a fixed
game configuration (BTN vs BB, 100\,bb, A$\heartsuit$K$\spadesuit$7$\diamondsuit$
flop, 50-bucket card abstraction, 3 bet sizes). Exploitability is measured
every $50$ iterations.

\textbf{Expected results.} CFR$^+$ reaches $\leq 5\%$ exploitability in
approximately $200$--$300$ iterations; vanilla CFR requires approximately
$800$--$1{,}000$ iterations for the same threshold.

\textbf{Figure~\ref{fig:cfr_vs_cfr+} (placeholder).} Exploitability vs.\
iterations for CFR and CFR$^+$ on a log-scale $y$-axis. CFR$^+$ convergence
is approximately $O(1/T)$ empirically vs.\ $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ for
CFR---consistent with \citet{tammelin2014}'s observations.

\subsection{Effect of abstraction granularity on convergence rate}

We run CFR$^+$ to convergence (exploitability $\leq 1\%$) under varying
card-abstraction bucket counts and measure: (a) iterations to convergence,
(b) wall-clock time to convergence, and (c) final exploitability at
iteration $1{,}000$.

\textbf{Expected results.} Coarser abstractions converge in fewer iterations
(smaller game tree) but plateau at higher exploitability. $50$ buckets
provides a good efficiency--quality tradeoff.
